A little about me

I start with a question.
Then I listen.

I’m Cindy, a UX researcher who brings a psychology lens and a mix of methods to understanding people.

The “why” is where I begin.

What makes a simple task feel difficult? Whose needs get missed? What happens after an experience ends? These are the kinds of questions that pull me into research.

My background in psychology and social science helps me explore behavior in context. I combine qualitative inquiry with quantitative analysis to understand both individual experiences and broader patterns.

I’m comfortable navigating ambiguity. At Google Consumer Payments, my internship project began with just a title: “Gen Z, AI, and Payments.” I turned that open-ended starting point into research questions and a mixed-methods study, then translated the evidence into opportunities and a research roadmap for product partners.

I combined diary research, interviews, and a survey, and shared the work with cross-functional teams and senior leadership. In our Workday project, I also helped turn scattered student feedback into a focused direction for improving navigation and search. I care about making research useful: a clearer design decision, a better feedback process, or a more informed use of resources.

I’m now studying Integrated Innovation for Products & Services at Carnegie Mellon, a program spanning design, engineering, and business. I’m focusing on UX research, with an interest in product insights, customer experience, and consulting roles where understanding people informs strategy.
